# Capability Module: Quantum Computing
## 1. Principles
Quantum Computing is treated within AING™ as a forward-looking, research-oriented capability. The guiding principles are problem-suitability and hybrid-computation. We recognize that quantum computers are not replacements for classical computers but are powerful accelerators for a specific class of problems (e.g., optimization, simulation, cryptography). The principle of hybrid computation emphasizes integrating quantum processors with classical systems.
## 2. Practices
The current practice of Quantum Computing in AING™ is focused on exploration and readiness. This includes identifying business problems that are intractable for classical computers but suitable for quantum algorithms (Initialize), using cloud-based quantum simulators and real quantum hardware for experimentation (Orchestrate), developing quantum algorithms (Co-Create), benchmarking their performance against classical alternatives (Validate), and building a "quantum-ready" workforce (Evolve).
## 3. Roles
- **Quantum Algorithm Researcher (Creator):** A highly specialized role for individuals with a background in quantum physics and computer science who design quantum algorithms.
- **Research Scientist (Strategist):** Identifies and frames the complex optimization or simulation problems that could benefit from quantum computation.
- **Classical Systems Integrator (Creator):** Focuses on building the hybrid systems that connect classical computers with quantum processing units (QPUs).
- **Post-Quantum Cryptography Guardian (Guardian):** A security role focused on preparing the organization's cryptographic standards for the day when quantum computers can break current encryption.
## 4. Governance
Governance for Quantum Computing is currently focused on investment and risk management. This includes managing a portfolio of research projects and partnerships. A key governance activity is assessing the risk that quantum computing poses to the organization's current cybersecurity infrastructure and developing a mitigation roadmap.
## 5. Tools
- **Quantum Development Kits (QDKs):** (e.g., Qiskit, Cirq) Software libraries for writing and testing quantum algorithms.
- **Cloud Quantum Platforms:** Services from providers like IBM, Google, and AWS that provide access to both quantum simulators and real quantum hardware.
- **Quantum-Inspired Optimization Solvers:** Classical software that uses principles from quantum mechanics to solve complex optimization problems on classical hardware.
## 6. Maturity Levels
1.  **Initial:** General awareness and basic research into quantum computing.
2.  **Managed:** Actively experimenting with quantum algorithms on cloud-based simulators.
3.  **Automated:** Running small-scale experiments on real quantum hardware.
4.  **Orchestrated:** A hybrid system where a classical AI Orchestrator can route specific, suitable sub-problems to a QPU for acceleration.
5.  **Co-Intelligent:** Quantum-enhanced AI models that can solve problems beyond the capability of classical AI.
