# Capability Module: Blockchain
## 1. Principles
Blockchain within the AING™ framework is approached with the principles of decentralization, immutability, and transparency. It is viewed as a specialized tool for scenarios requiring a high degree of trust and auditability among multiple, independent parties. The principle of "appropriate use" is key; blockchain is not a solution for every problem.
## 2. Practices
AING™ advocates for the use of permissioned (private) blockchains for most enterprise use cases. Practices include identifying use cases where a decentralized ledger adds unique value (Initialize), designing and deploying smart contracts to automate multi-party agreements (Orchestrate/Co-Create), integrating the blockchain with existing enterprise systems (Co-Create), auditing the blockchain for security and performance (Validate), and managing the governance of the decentralized network (Evolve).
## 3. Roles
- **Blockchain Developer (Creator):** Writes, tests, and deploys smart contracts.
- **Business Consortium Lead (Strategist):** Works with multiple organizations to define the rules and governance of the shared blockchain.
- **Smart Contract Auditor (Guardian):** A specialized security role that audits smart contracts for vulnerabilities before they are deployed.
- **AI Oracle (AI Agent):** An AI agent that can securely provide real-world data to a smart contract.
## 4. Governance
Blockchain governance in AING™ is about managing the rules of the decentralized network. This includes defining the consensus mechanism, the process for onboarding new participants, and the procedure for updating smart contracts. For permissioned blockchains, a formal governance body comprising all participants is typically required.
## 5. Tools
- **Smart Contract Development Framework:** (e.g., Hardhat, Truffle) for building and testing smart contracts.
- **Blockchain Explorer:** A tool for visualizing and inspecting transactions and data on the blockchain.
- **Decentralized Identity (DID) System:** A system for managing the identities of participants in the network in a secure and verifiable way.
## 6. Maturity Levels
1.  **Initial:** Experimenting with public blockchains or simple proof-of-concept projects.
2.  **Managed:** Deploying a private, permissioned blockchain for a single, specific use case (e.g., supply chain tracking).
3.  **Automated:** Using smart contracts to automate complex, multi-party business processes.
4.  **Orchestrated:** Integrating AI agents (oracles) with the blockchain to trigger smart contracts based on real-world events.
5.  **Co-Intelligent:** A Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) where AI agents and humans collaborate to govern and operate a business process on the blockchain.
## 7. Certification Pathway
Blockchain is an advanced, specialized topic covered as part of the **AING™ Architect Certificate (AAC)** for professionals designing systems that require decentralized trust.
