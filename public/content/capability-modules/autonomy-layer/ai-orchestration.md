# Capability Module: AI Orchestration
## 1. Principles
AI Orchestration is the core of the AING™ framework itself, elevated to a formal capability. Its principles are dynamic adaptation, holistic optimization, and human-on-the-loop control. The AI Orchestrator does not simply automate a fixed process; it intelligently manages a fluid workflow, making real-time decisions to achieve a strategic outcome. Humans set the goals and constraints, and the AI determines the optimal path.
## 2. Practices
The practice of AI Orchestration involves defining a strategic outcome in the AING™ Canvas (Initialize), allowing the AI Orchestrator to generate a dynamic plan and allocate resources (Orchestrate), monitoring the progress on the AING™ Flowboard as human-AI teams execute tasks (Co-Create), reviewing AI-generated performance insights (Validate), and allowing the Orchestrator to re-plan and re-allocate resources based on those insights (Evolve).
## 3. Roles
- **AI Orchestrator (AI Agent):** The master AI agent that manages the end-to-end workflow.
- **Strategist (Human Role):** Defines the goals, constraints, and success metrics that guide the Orchestrator.
- **Guardian (Human Role):** Oversees the Orchestrator, ensuring its decisions align with ethical and strategic guidelines, and intervenes when necessary.
- **All Creators (Human & AI):** Act as resources that the Orchestrator can allocate to tasks.
## 4. Governance
Governance of the AI Orchestrator is a primary function of the Guardian role. Humans must define the "rules of engagement" for the Orchestrator, including its budget, its authority to re-allocate resources, and the conditions under which it must seek human approval. The Orchestrator's decision-making process must be transparent and auditable.
## 5. Tools
- **AING™ Canvas:** The primary tool for providing strategic direction to the Orchestrator.
- **AING™ Flowboard:** The primary interface for visualizing the output and current state of the Orchestrator's plan.
- **Orchestrator Configuration Panel:** An interface for Guardians to set and adjust the operational parameters and ethical constraints of the Orchestrator.
## 6. Maturity Levels
1.  **Initial:** Using simple workflow automation tools (e.g., Zapier, IFTTT).
2.  **Managed:** Using Business Process Management (BPM) software with fixed, human-designed workflows.
3.  **Automated:** Using an AI Orchestrator to manage a single, well-defined process (e.g., customer onboarding).
4.  **Orchestrated:** Using an AI Orchestrator to manage complex, cross-functional projects or programs.
5.  **Co-Intelligent:** An enterprise-wide "meta-Orchestrator" that manages the entire portfolio of company initiatives to optimize for global strategic goals.
## 7. Certification Pathway
Understanding AI Orchestration is fundamental to the entire AING™ curriculum. It is introduced in the **AING™ Foundation Certificate (AFC)** and is a central theme in the **Practitioner (APC)**, **Architect (AAC)**, and **Leader (ALC)** certifications.
